# Review, November 2026: combining `refactor` into `main`

This note records how the `refactor` branch was brought onto `main`, what was cleaned up on the way, and what a critical read of the methodology found.
It is the combine-and-review step only; a general review of the analysis and an assessment of whether any of it is usable in live trading come separately, after this lands.

## 3. Methodology findings, most important first

### 3.1 The risk-neutral skewness is not comparable with Cboe's SKEW (described, not changed; the top recommendation for the follow-up review)

`validation.risk_neutral_skewness`, used for the Cboe SKEW comparison, integrates the log-return moments over the whole fitted support, down to about zero.
The third moment of `log(S/F)` is extremely sensitive to mass far below the forward: `log(0.1) ≈ -2.3`, so one per cent of mass between `0.1F` and `0.3F` moves the log-skewness of a thirty-day density by more than five units (a test shows exactly that).
That mass is below the lowest traded strike, so it comes from the roughness penalty and the three tail knots, not from any price.

On the last real-data run this showed up as a log-skewness of **-8.2** for a typical fitted density, and a SKEW-equivalent averaging **45 index points** above Cboe's (correlation 0.30).
So the full-support number is not like-for-like with Cboe's: it is dominated by extrapolated mass.

Truncating the density to the traded strikes and renormalising is not like-for-like either.
Cboe computes SKEW with the Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan method, which integrates out-of-the-money option *prices* over the traded strikes.
The put at the lowest traded strike already prices all the probability below that strike, so Cboe's number keeps the left tail that truncation would drop, and a truncated skewness would be biased toward zero.

The like-for-like comparison is to compute the Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan moments from the fitted spline's call and put prices over the traded strikes.
That is a change to the validation methodology, so it is left for the follow-up review, where it is the first thing to do.
Until then, the SKEW comparison in the notebook says little about the recovered shape, and the log-skewness in the tilt-moment summary carries the same caveat.

### 3.2 The external rate and dividend checks ran on chains the analysis rejects (fixed)

The implied-rate and implied-dividend comparisons were computed on every chain with a parity fit, including the 115 that fail the quality gates, with implied rates from -661% to +3,332%.
Pearson correlation and mean bias are not robust to that: the last run reported correlations of **0.006, 0.044 and 0.004** with Treasury bills at 30, 60 and 90 days, mean biases of **150–290 bp**, and a by-year bias of 1,945 bp in 2010 (a single outlier of 33.3 among 175 chains contributes 1,900 bp).
The dividend yield showed a correlation of **0.12** and a bias of 183 bp.

The notebook nevertheless told the reader that "the level, the shape and the turning points come out of option prices alone".

Fix: both checks now use `analysis_sample`, the chains that pass the gates.
**This needs a re-run before anything is concluded.**
Even after the outlier cleanup, two things deserve a look.
First, the per-year biases in quiet years (about 150 bp in 2005–2007 and 60–70 bp in 2014–2016 on the ungated data) look larger than the few tens of basis points by which SPX box-spread rates usually exceed bill rates.
Second, whatever is producing the extreme implied rates in 2009–2010 may also be affecting chains that pass the gates.

### 3.3 Some of the notebook's prose was written ahead of its results (partly fixed)

Several narrative passages asserted results the outputs did not show.
The rate check was described as successful while its correlation was near zero, as above.
The rate and dividend cells cited behaviour "in 2019" and "in 2020", which are excluded years and not in the sample.
The conclusion said the risk-neutral densities "fail in the direction the theory predicts", while the risk-neutral PIT on one observation per expiration had a KS p-value of 0.34 pooled and 0.10, 0.22 and 0.02 by horizon.
And the conclusion template would have printed "-20% of the gap is location" (on the last run, recentring the baseline on the forward made it slightly *worse*).

The passages above have been rewritten to say what each check can show, and to size the calibration claim to the evidence.
After the next real-data run, every printed interpretation and markdown cell should be re-read against the numbers above it.

### 3.4 The restricted-tilt robustness check had a normalisation bug (fixed)

With `tilt_range` set, the CRRA weight was `(s / m)^gamma` inside the range, where `m` is the median of the strike grid, and exactly `1` outside it.
The relative weight of the inside and the outside therefore depended on an arbitrary normalising constant, and the tilted density jumped at both ends of the range.
The kernel is now held at its boundary value outside the range, which is continuous and does not depend on `m`.
The restricted estimate (1.75 against 1.58 on the last run) will change.

### 3.5 Gamma cannot be estimated by minimising CRPS (described, not changed)

On the real data the 95% paired-bootstrap interval for gamma is [0, 5], the whole grid.
On the synthetic market, where the outcomes are drawn at a gamma of 2.5, the estimate is 2.2 and the interval is again [0, 5], although that is not a clean known-truth test (section 3.6).
`evaluation.crps_location_sensitivity` explains why: the tilt moves the mean by `gamma sigma^2 tau`, which changes the expected CRPS by about one per cent.
So Part 3, as designed, cannot answer its own question with any sample this size.
Two alternatives would change results materially and are left as decisions:

- estimate gamma by maximum likelihood, scoring the tilted density's log value at the outcome (the log score is far more sensitive to location and tails than CRPS); or
- estimate it on a training period and evaluate on held-out years, which also removes the in-sample problem the README already acknowledges.

### 3.6 The synthetic demo's known gamma is known for one chain per expiration only (described, not changed)

`synthetic.generate_sample` draws one settlement outcome per expiration, from the CRRA tilt of the middle model observed for that expiration (roughly a mid-window 60-day chain).
Every other chain of that expiration, at every horizon, is scored against that draw, but each has its own forward (from a different point on the index path), its own volatility and its own maturity.
Only the source chain's density is an exact tilt of the outcome distribution.
So gamma-hat and the PIT calibration, which the demo pools across all chains and horizons, are confounded by forecast misspecification: a gamma near 2.5, or a uniform PIT, is not what a correct estimator must produce there.

Recommended for the follow-up review, simplest first:

- restrict the gamma-recovery and PIT checks to the chain each outcome was drawn from, which makes them valid known-truth tests at the cost of sample size; or
- let the index path drive both the chains and the outcomes, so that every chain's density is an exact description of its own outcome.

### 3.7 Tests did not pass on the refactor branch (fixed)

Six of 88 tests failed.
Five used a test fixture whose mid-prices were perturbed by up to one index point while quoting a half-point spread, so they correctly failed the new parity gate; the fixture now quotes a spread that covers its own noise.
One expected the CRPS of a point mass to be zero, which is not what the exact quadrature computes on a finite grid (it is `h / 3`); the test now checks that value.
Once the fixture was fixed, two more failed because the ADMM fallback solver did not converge on the wider-spread chains; it was removed (section 2).
New tests cover the sensitivity of the full-support skewness to extrapolated mass, the continuity of the restricted tilt, `score_chain` against a known density, and the synthetic dataset round trip.

### 3.8 Smaller points (described, not changed)

- **Hidden configuration (since fixed).**
  `config.ROUGHNESS_PENALTY` was read from `data/processed/selected_roughness_penalty.json` at import time, so the default penalty of every function depended on a file outside the repository.
  The import-time read has since been dropped, and the penalty is passed explicitly to every fit.
- **The one-standard-error rule** in `selection.select_roughness_penalty` compares *median* held-out errors against the standard error of the *mean*.
  The standard error of a median is about 25% larger, so the rule is slightly less conservative than it says.
  It made no difference on the last run (0.1, 1 and 10 are all within one standard error, and 10 was chosen).
- **Newey–West on small panels.**
  The pooled lag is the 90-day horizon's 63 trading days times the rows per date.
  On the full sample that is 189 rows against about 6,800, which is fine, but the demo's 145-row lag on 237 rows triggers the code's own warning.
  The block bootstrap is the number to believe there, as the notebook says.
- **FITPACK warnings.**
  On a few chains `make_splrep` reports that it could not reach the smoothing bound `s = n` exactly ("s too small").
  The implied-volatility baseline is then slightly rougher than intended on those chains; counting them, as `n_smile_knots` already allows, would show whether it matters.
- **Parity tolerance naming.**
  The quality gate compared each parity residual with the sum of the call and put half-spreads but called it the "average half-spread".
  The quantity was right; the name and the comments now say what it is.
- **`tests/test_review_fixes.py`** is organised by issue numbers ("2.1", "2.2", ...) from a review document that is not in the repository.
  Moving its tests next to the modules they test would make them easier to find.

### What checked out

- The Breeden–Litzenberger implementation in normalised coordinates, the boundary conditions, and the two identities they imply (unit mass, mean equal to the parity forward) are correct, and are tested on every fit.
- Imposing `g'' >= 0` at the breakpoints is sufficient for a cubic spline, since `g''` is linear between them.
- The CRPS quadrature is exact for the piecewise-linear interpolant of the tabulated CDF and is tested against the Gaussian and lognormal closed forms.
- Put–call parity recovers the forward and rate exactly on clean data, and to within 0.05% under quote noise.
- No outcome information leaks into the density fits: the roughness penalty is chosen on held-out *quotes*, the lognormal benchmark uses the same chain's at-the-money volatility, and settlement values are joined by expiration month only.
  The only in-sample step is gamma, which the notebook already flags.
- The dependence structure (overlapping daily forecasts, three horizons per outcome) is handled carefully for score comparisons: a HAC lag in rows, and a bootstrap over expiration months.
  One observation per expiration was not enough for the 60- and 90-day calibration tests, whose forecast windows still overlap; they now use non-overlapping expirations (`stats.non_overlapping_panel`).

## 4. Open questions

1. **SKEW.** Replace the density-moment skewness with Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan moments computed from the fitted spline's prices over the traded strikes (section 3.1)?
2. **Part 3.** Should gamma be re-estimated by maximum likelihood, by a train/test split by year, or both (section 3.5)?
   And should the synthetic demo's gamma and PIT checks be restricted to each outcome's source chain, or driven by the index path (section 3.6)?
3. **Data quality in 2009–2010.** What produces implied rates of several hundred per cent there, and does it also affect chains that pass the gates (section 3.2)?
4. **Re-run.** The validation, SKEW, tilt-summary, restricted-tilt and conclusion cells need the licensed data; their new outputs should be checked against the expectations in section 3.
5. **Benchmarks.** `benchmarks.climatology_forecast` exists but is not used.
   A time-series benchmark (the empirical distribution of past returns, strictly before each forecast date) is what would show whether the options add information beyond history.
   That matters more for any practical use than the 1% between the two smoothers.
6. **The organisation of `test_review_fixes.py`** (section 3.8): worth simplifying?
   The configuration file read at import time has since been removed.
